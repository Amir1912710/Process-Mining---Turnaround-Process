\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
This thesis investigates airport turnaround analysis under limited operational observations. The primary empirical collection contains 1,322 departure records from the Berlin Brandenburg Airport A-CDM application. OpenSky provides an independent departure-time cross-check rather than the source of these primary rows. Among 1,286 eligible actual takeoffs, 1,257 have a unique callsign/time correspondence candidate, and 1,237 of those agree within one minute. Target-to-takeoff differences describe departure values but do not measure ground-service durations. A separate earlier study used an auxiliary OpenSky reconstruction to adapt an object-centric airport simulation and analyze its generated logs in Promenade. Its 60 repeated runs remain conditional pilot evidence and appear separately in the appendices. The 10,940 reconstructed aircraft intervals are not A-CDM observations. The primary A-CDM collection lacks arrival/block milestones and internal service timestamps, which prevents a claim of full turnaround calibration from those records alone. The contribution is an auditable empirical collection, an explicit correspondence method and a documented exploratory simulation, with their sources and limits kept distinct.
